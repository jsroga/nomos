# George R.R. Martin Writing Techniques

## The Gardener vs Architect Approach

GRRM famously describes himself as a "gardener" rather than an "architect":

> "I think there are two types of writers, the architects and the gardeners. The architects plan everything ahead of time... The gardeners dig a hole, drop in a seed and water it... And I'm much more a gardener than an architect."

### What This Means for Story Generation

1. **Let characters surprise you** - Don't force them into predetermined paths
2. **Organic plot development** - Story emerges from character decisions
3. **Be willing to change direction** - If a better path reveals itself, take it
4. **Trust the process** - Sometimes you won't know where you're going

## POV Discipline

Each POV character should:
- Have their own distinct voice
- See the world through their biases
- Not know things they couldn't know
- Have their own internal logic and goals

### POV Rules
- No head-hopping within a scene
- Limited knowledge creates tension
- Unreliable narrators add depth
- Physical sensations ground the reader

## The "Valar Morghulis" Principle

"All men must die" - but deaths must be:
- **Earned** - Foreshadowed, not random
- **Meaningful** - Changes the story's direction
- **Consequential** - Affects other characters
- **Sometimes mundane** - Heroes can die to arrows, not just epic battles

## Gray Morality Scale

Characters exist on a spectrum:
```
Pure Evil ←——————————→ Pure Good
    ↑                        ↑
 (Almost none)        (Almost none)

Most characters live here:
    ←—[Complex Middle Ground]—→
```

## Worldbuilding Through Implication

- History should feel lived-in
- Don't info-dump; reveal through character actions
- Let the world have secrets the reader never learns
- Food, clothing, and customs matter

## Subverting Expectations

The GRRM way:
1. Set up a trope expectation
2. Make it seem inevitable
3. Plant subtle seeds of alternative
4. Subvert at the right moment
5. Make it feel earned, not cheap
